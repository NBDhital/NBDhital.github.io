Research Area

Air Pollution, Exposure & Health

Mobile source emissions characterization: A major part of my research centers on real-world mobile source emissions measurement using Portable Emissions Measurement Systems (PEMS). PEMS enables the quantification of emissions under real-world operating conditions, providing a more realistic quantification of emissions. Since my doctoral studies at Chaoyang University of Technology (CYUT), Taiwan, I have been actively collaborating with the CYUT Air Pollution Research Group on several PEMS-based studies. We have characterized emissions from a wide range of emission sources, including on-road vehicles, inland vessels, and off-road construction machinery.

We have investigated both particulate and gaseous emissions, including particulate matter, carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon dioxide, total hydrocarbons, volatile organic compounds and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and their relationships with driving behavior, vehicle operating conditions, route attributes, fuel types, and emission control technologies. Our PEMS studies have generated comprehensive databases of real-world emission factors and chemical profiles, contributing to improved emission inventories, source apportionment studies, and the development of effective emission control strategies.

Characterizing residential combustion emissions: Building on my expertise in emissions characterization, I have conducted extensive research on residential wood combustion and other small-scale household combustion sources, which are significant contributors to air pollution in many parts of the world, including South Asia. My research focuses on the characterization of aerosol emissions, including particle size distributions, chemical composition, volatile organic compounds, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, as well as the evaluation of emission reduction technologies. This work supports a better understanding of household combustion emissions and provides scientific evidence for measures aimed at reducing both ambient and indoor air pollution and their associated health effects.



Exposure assessment: In addition to emissions characterization, it is equally important to understand how people are exposed to air pollutants in their daily lives. My research therefore also examines personal exposure to air pollutants, particularly in in transportation microenvironments. Through studies conducted in Nepal, Hong Kong, and Taiwan, I have evaluated exposure to particulate matter, black carbon, VOCs, and other gaseous pollutants across a variety of transportation microenvironments. These studies provide insights into the factors influencing exposure and help identify practical measures for reducing population exposure.


By integrating real-world emissions characterization, and exposure studies, I aim to advance scientific understanding of air pollution and contribute to the development of effective solutions for cleaner air and healthier communities. I welcome opportunities to collaborate with researchers, policymakers, and organizations interested in advancing air quality science and developing evidence-based approaches to emission control and exposure mitigation.
