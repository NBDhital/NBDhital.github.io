Narayan Babu Dhital, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor · Department of Environmental Science
Patan Multiple Campus, Tribhuvan University, Nepal

Growing up in the rural mountains of Nepal, I was accustomed to remarkably clear visibility, with the Himalayas and surrounding forest-covered mountains forming a constant backdrop to daily life. Surrounded by clean air and breathtaking landscapes, I rarely thought about the value of the air I breathed. Only after moving to Kathmandu for higher education did I realize that clean air, which I had long taken for granted, was in fact a precious resource. Breathing the city's air often felt suffocating, in sharp contrast to the fresh mountain air that defined my childhood.

Over time, I witnessed a troubling reality: air pollution was no longer confined to urban centers such as Kathmandu. The problem had spread far beyond the city. The mountain views I grew up with are often obscured by haze, and clear blue skies have become increasingly rare. What was once an urban environmental problem has evolved into a regional crisis. The haze that obscures mountain views is only the most visible manifestation of a much larger problem. In Nepal, air pollution contributes to more than one-fifth of all deaths and is the leading risk factor for all-cause mortality. Addressing this challenge requires a robust scientific understanding of emission sources, pollutant transport and atmospheric processes, human exposure, health impacts, and broader environmental consequences. Such knowledge is essential for developing effective policies and evidence-based mitigation strategies. Driven by a vision of restoring clean air across Nepal and South Asia, I investigate the sources, dynamics, and impacts of atmospheric pollution, with a focus on emission characterization and their effects on air quality and human exposure. Beyond research, I am committed to mentoring future scientists and fostering collaborations that connect science, policy, and practice to advance cleaner air and healthier communities.

Research interest: Real-World Emission Characterization; Exposure and Health Impact Assessment.
