DATA SOURCE

Where These Data Come From

The live readings displayed above come from the Open-Meteo air quality API, which uses outputs from atmospheric models, such as the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, to estimate air pollutant concentrations at any location, updated hourly. Please note that it is a modeled estimate, not a direct observation. Accuracy is not guaranteed. Visit the webpage of Open-Meteo for more information about the data source.

For Nepal's official air quality monitoring data, visit the Department of Environment's real-time monitoring portal.

Air Quality Index

Understanding the AQI

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a standardized scale, used widely around the world, that translates air pollutant concentrations into a single number from 0 to 500+, making it easy to understand whether the air is healthy. The overall AQI shown above is the worst of six individual pollutant sub-indices (PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO2, SO2, and O3). The AQI is classified into six categories, each defined by a specific pollutant concentration range and accompanied by a corresponding health advisory. A higher AQI means a greater level of air pollution and a greater health concern.

Note that the overall AQI reported above is calculated based on rolling-average concentrations. Therefore, current concentration readings may not match the overall AQI score.
